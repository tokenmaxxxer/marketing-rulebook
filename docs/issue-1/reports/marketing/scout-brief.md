# Scout Brief — marketing domain methodology survey (Phase 1, issue-1)

Subject: issue-1

Status: Phase 1 (research). No live web search available in this
environment; this brief draws on trained knowledge of well-documented,
canonical, widely-published marketing frameworks (textbook/industry-
standard, not obscure or single-source). Every claim below is attributed
to a named framework/author/book as its source.

## Must-bes (frameworks any adopted methodology must not contradict)

- A positioning/messaging method must force **competitive-alternatives-
  first** thinking, not "we're great" assertion. Source: April Dunford,
  *Obviously Awesome* (2019), positioning canvas — 10 components
  (competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value/benefits, target
  segment characteristics, market category, relevant trends), explicitly
  starting from "what would the customer use instead of us."
- A channel plan must distinguish **testing** candidate channels from
  **committing** to one, not jump straight to a "the channel." Source:
  Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares, *Traction* (2015), Bullseye Framework
  — 19 possible channels, phase 1 brainstorm all, phase 2 run cheap
  parallel tests on the most-promising ~3, phase 3 focus resources on the
  one that works.
- A target-segment definition must state **criteria used**, not just a
  label. Source: classic STP model (Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning),
  widely attributed to Wendell Smith (1956) segmentation origin and
  formalized as STP in modern marketing-management textbooks (e.g. Kotler
  & Keller, *Marketing Management*); segmentation criteria families are
  firmographic, demographic, behavioral, psychographic.

## Performance axes (what distinguishes a good artifact from a weak one)

1. **Falsifiability of the positioning claim.** StoryBrand (Donald
   Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*, 2017) frames messaging as the
   customer being the hero and the brand the guide — useful for
   narrative clarity, but its 7-part framework does not itself force
   competitive differentiation the way Dunford's canvas does. Both are
   legitimate; Dunford's is stronger on falsifiability (it requires
   naming real alternatives customers would otherwise pick), StoryBrand
   is stronger on narrative usability for copywriting.
2. **Segment definition tied to jobs, not just demographics.**
   Jobs-to-be-Done (Clayton Christensen; popularized by Bob Moesta / Tony
   Ulwick, *"Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice"*) argues segments should
   be defined by the job the customer is "hiring" the product to do, as a
   complement/corrective to purely demographic segmentation, which can
   group people who behave nothing alike.
3. **Channel classification completeness.** RACE framework (Smart
   Insights / Dave Chaffey, digital-marketing planning literature) —
   Reach, Act, Convert, Engage — is a full-funnel planning lens broader
   than channel selection alone; useful as a check that a channel plan
   states what stage of the funnel each channel serves, not just which
   channel. Paid/owned/earned taxonomy (widely used in industry practice,
   e.g. Forrester's paid/owned/earned media model) is the minimum
   classification any channel plan should apply per candidate channel.
4. **Evidence over assertion.** All of the above frameworks explicitly
   expect claims to rest on gathered evidence, not opinion: Dunford's
   canvas is built from structured **customer/win-loss interviews**;
   Bullseye's phase-2 test step is explicitly an **empirical, cheap-test**
   step before committing budget; STP/ICP practice (see below) expects
   **market/firmographic data**, not assumption. None of these frameworks
   treat an un-cited assertion as adequate.

## Adopt / skip decisions

- **Adopt**: April Dunford's positioning canvas as the required method
  for the messaging-doc field. Reason: strongest fit for a role whose
  declared decision is "which message to which channel" — the canvas is
  built around comparison (why us over the alternative), which is
  exactly the decision this role owns. StoryBrand is a reasonable
  complementary copywriting lens but is not adopted as the *required*
  method, to avoid requiring two frameworks where one suffices; the
  proposal names Dunford's canvas as primary and may reference StoryBrand
  as an optional narrative pass.
- **Adopt**: Bullseye Framework's 3-phase test as the required method for
  the channel-plan field. Reason: directly matches this role's
  write_scope of `[]` (it plans/tests, does not execute/own
  implementation) and produces a natural hand-off to growth-analytics —
  Bullseye's "what worked" step is exactly the input growth-analytics
  needs to interpret funnel performance.
- **Adopt**: STP + ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) as the required method for
  the target-segment field, with JTBD as a secondary/complementary
  criterion inside "behavioral" segmentation, not a full requirement.
  Reason: STP is the industry-standard, most widely taught model and
  covers demographic/firmographic/behavioral/psychographic criteria as a
  checklist; JTBD is best used as *one* possible behavioral lens rather
  than mandated wholesale, to keep the requirement lightweight for a
  planning-only role.
- **Skip (for now)**: RACE as a mandatory structure. Reason: RACE is a
  full customer-lifecycle planning framework (broader than "which
  message to which channel"); mandating it fully would push this role's
  produces toward funnel-stage ownership, which oversteps into
  growth-analytics's territory per the stated hand-off. Recommend using
  paid/owned/earned classification (simpler, narrower) as the mandatory
  minimum, and note RACE only as an optional richer lens a phase-2
  executor may choose to reference.
- **Skip**: full Van Westendorp/pricing-research methods, and full
  competitive-intelligence tooling frameworks — out of scope for this
  role's declared decision boundary (pricing and deep competitive intel
  belong to other roles per this repo's role taxonomy, not marketing's
  "which message to which channel").

## Gap line: what the current 3-field norm covers vs. misses vs. the field

- Covers: correct field *names* — `messaging-doc`, `channel-plan`,
  `target-segment` map cleanly onto positioning-canvas output, Bullseye
  output, and STP/ICP output respectively. No renaming needed.
- Misses: any methodology pointer, any required sub-components, any
  evidence-citation requirement, any explicit tie to the growth-analytics
  hand-off (e.g., no requirement that channel-plan or target-segment
  carry measurable/falsifiable success criteria).
- Vs. the field: every framework surveyed above treats "assertion without
  evidence" as inadequate output; this repo's current gate can only check
  field *presence*, not evidence or structure — a material gap the
  maturation proposal should flag as aspirational/future rather than
  claim as immediately enforceable.
